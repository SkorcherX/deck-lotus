Theme forge

Make a Deck Lotus theme, one step at a time. No terminal. Nothing to remember — each step tells you exactly what to do and checks the result before letting you move on.

    Step 1 of 7

    Name your theme

    The name is what you will see in the theme picker. The slug is the folder it lives in — lowercase, no spaces. It is generated for you, so you can ignore it.

    Filled in from the name. Change it only if you have a reason.

    Shown under the name in the picker.

    Step 2 of 7

    Describe the picture you want

    One or two sentences. Name a place, a weather condition and a light source. Light is the part the colour extractor reads, so being concrete about it is what decides whether your theme gets a palette or a puddle.

    Not sure? Start from one of these and edit it

    Good: “Sunken Amonkhet necropolis at noon; heat haze over bleached sandstone; hard gold light and long violet shadows.”

    Too vague to extract from: “Blue and magical, kind of cool looking.”

    Step 3 of 7

    Pick how dark the app should be

    This is the flat colour behind every page — and, more importantly, the exact colour your artwork will be told to fade into at its edges. Choosing it now, before any art exists, is the whole trick: art asked to fade to “near-black” comes back a few shades off and shows as a lighter stripe down the side of the window.

    Colour family

    All the way left is a true neutral black. All the way right is as tinted as a background can be before it starts competing with the artwork.

    Before you move on, check three things

    • Is the middle quiet enough that white page titles will read over it?
    • Is it genuinely dark — no bright sky filling the whole band?
    • Did it smuggle in any lettering? Image models love adding fake runes. Regenerate if so.

    Step 5 of 7

    Turn the banner into a palette

    Drop the banner back in. This reads the artwork, builds a palette from it, corrects that palette against the accessibility rules, and checks that the art's edges actually fade to the background colour you picked.

    Does the art meet the page?

    What the artwork contains

    Clustered in OKLCH, not HSL — HSL lightness is inconsistent across hues and will confidently hand you a “dark” violet that is visually glaring.

    Your corrected palette

    The artwork donates hue and colourfulness only. Depth is forced onto a fixed ladder so art always separates from content, and text is solved for contrast rather than sampled — a colour taken from a painting has no idea what it will end up sitting on.

    Step 6 of 7

    Make the rest of the art

    Now that the colours are fixed, these prompts carry your real hex values. Do them one at a time, attaching your banner to each one as a reference image — five pictures generated separately from the same sentence look like five ideas, not one theme.

    Every one of these is optional. A theme with only a banner works fine; the rest of the chrome just stays plain. Skip any you do not want and it will be left out of the manifest.

    2 · Register it

    Paste this into the THEMES list in client/src/themes/registry.js. Without it the theme cannot be selected.

    3 · Add the slug to the pre-paint list

    In client/index.html, find the line starting var KNOWN = and add your slug to it. Without this, anyone who picks your theme sees one frame of the default theme on every page load.

    4 · Rebuild and look at it

    Rebuild the client, then pick your theme in Settings. Walk a chart-heavy page (Deck Builder, Cards, Price Monitoring) — a grey or wrong-coloured chart means a hardcoded colour escaped somewhere and is worth reporting. Rails only appear on windows wider than 1700px, so check at the screen and scaling you actually use.
